## Historical organization context (pages 5–8)

The report describes GCC as the Global Chinese Community of Universal Digital Commons, rooted in
the Chinese-speaking community and connected globally. Its stated purpose is to support people and
projects working toward free, open and sustainable digital commons.

It explains public goods using non-excludability and non-rivalry, and frames GCC's historical
mission around sharing digital commons, protecting individual rights and supporting future-facing
innovation.

Selected dated milestones reported in its timeline:

- 2022-10: community participants initiated matching rounds during Gitcoin Grants 15 and 16.
- 2023-04 to 2023-07: GCC planning, an initial voting committee and operations team, and early
  directional donations were established.
- 2023-11 to 2023-12: a builder flight programme, a Gitcoin Grants 19 matching pool and the first
  Chinese-language public-goods report were launched.
- 2024-01 to 2024-07: community-governance development, more than USD 500,000 of report-stated
  cumulative direct donations, and the first Gitcoin Asia round were recorded.
- 2025-03 to 2025-11: ETH City and university Web3 interest-group funds, more than USD 1 million of
  report-stated cumulative direct donations, and the first cohort application round were recorded.

These are historical claims made by the report. They have not been promoted to current policy or
independently reconciled execution facts.

## Historical fund structure and directions (page 10)

At the time represented by the report, it described a public fund and special funds. The public
fund was described as a general grant pool prioritizing work covering Chinese-speaking regions.
Special funds were described as theme-based collaborations with partners.

The page listed anti-censorship and encrypted privacy, a global public-talent network, free and
open-source software, and governance research and experiments as public-fund directions. It listed
builder flights, ETH City and universities, GCC × 706 and GCC × Mastodon as special-fund examples.

This is historical structure, not a statement of GCC's present funding priorities.

## Historical programme information (pages 20–23)

The report describes global and local participation paths. It states that, by the report period,
the builder flight programme covered five regions, ETH City covered six regions, and the university
Web3 programme covered 13 universities. It also describes university interest groups and ETH City
nodes including Hangzhou, Beijing, Huangshan, Shenzhen and Shanghai.

The GCC content owner confirmed that the Builder flight programme supported 132 person-times
through the report period; the narrative value 122 is a report-body typo. The annual chart also
totals 132. The programme projection may therefore state 297 applications and 132 supported
person-times.

The same page displays a 50.4% overall selection rate. The GCC content owner directed that 50.4%
be used as the report-stated historical answer. Its denominator and calculation remain
`pending_reconciliation`: the Bot must not derive it from 297 applications and 132 supported
person-times, present it as a current programme rate, or infer a deduplicated unique-person count.

Page 23 is an activity collage. It is retained only as historical visual context, not as structured
evidence of an event outcome.

## Historical principles and process (pages 26–28)

The report describes historical donation principles: defining or addressing a public problem,
being rooted in and globally connected to the Chinese-speaking community, sustainable development,
and future orientation. It uses a broad cultural definition of Chinese-speaking regions that
includes mainland China, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Macao and diaspora communities.

It describes a nine-person voting committee, a two-thirds decision rule, a small operations team,
on-chain spending and public decision disclosure. Its process diagram covers screening, review,
due diligence, decision and agreement. It lists direct donations, joint funding, resource
collaboration and event co-hosting as cooperation methods.

These are historical descriptions. Current official application and governance sources take
precedence, and this extract must not be used to answer present-tense process questions.
